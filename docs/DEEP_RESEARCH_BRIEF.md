2. Establish relevant keyword/local visibility opportunity through grids or comparable location-specific measurements.
3. Reach the owner or person authorized to decide on marketing.
4. Offer a qualifying no-charge ranking demonstration with clear scope and continuation terms.

## Required research areas

### 1. Demand and buyer motivations

Assess demand for paid Maps visibility services, buyer priorities, budget constraints, prior agency disappointment, skepticism, trust barriers, local search purchase behavior and willingness to try/pay. Use original data with limitations rather than recycled universal traffic/click percentages.

### 2. Existing agency models and competition

Find concrete current offers from agencies selling Maps ranking, local SEO, free trials, rank-first/pay-later, pay-on-results, or performance-based services. Compare exact scope, prices, trial length, payment trigger, guarantees, contracts, channels, proof, territory exclusivity and recurring value. Distinguish comparable narrow ranking services from broad SEO packages. Include success and failure experience, not only vendor landing pages.

Identify what would actually differentiate a new local owner-led agency and whether the market is crowded or commoditized. Do not treat a competitor's advertised result as independently proven performance.

### 3. Ideal customer and market choice

Compare 5–8 plausible verticals, including auto repair, specialty auto/body, HVAC, plumbing, roofing, dental and other credible physical-location services. Consider job contribution, repeat value, Maps intent, review parity, location suitability, competition, decision-maker accessibility, lead handling and capacity.

Recommend where Mitch should start, and explain which conclusions are evidence-backed versus reasoned hypotheses. Avoid generic “high ticket is best” advice. Investigate whether a narrower specialty/market improves reach and relevance.

### 4. Prospect discovery and qualification

Research methods for finding businesses that can benefit, not just businesses listed on Maps. Compare market-level competitor grids, individual scans, search/category discovery and enrichment. Specify a lean process and realistic time/cost at small scale, **without assuming provider-tool access**.

Use the PDF's trial eligibility as inputs. Investigate keyword intent and value, true local coverage, Top 3 opportunity, competitor review credibility, owner readiness and budget. Explain how to avoid attractive-looking low-value keywords, misleading average ranks and arbitrary score precision.

### 5. Reaching owners and acquiring clients

Assess direct calls, personalized email, short audit videos, in-person approaches, referrals, partnerships with web/marketing firms, trade networks and Meta advertising. Compare fit, time, cash cost, trust, scalability and evidence quality for a solo local agency.

Use practical local-business experience and credible practitioner/owner discussions. Separate cold outreach from inbound signup follow-up. Develop a decision-maker research process and testable messaging. Do not import enterprise SaaS reply/conversion benchmarks as forecasts for repair shops.

### 6. Free demonstration and trial-to-paid selling

Assess whether demonstrating a rank gain before purchase meaningfully reduces skepticism, what barriers remain, and how to design the trial so it attracts viable buyers rather than freebie seekers.

Specify qualification, owner agreement, scope, pre-trial price disclosure, baseline, results meeting, expectations, follow-up and continuation decision. Consider alternatives such as a free audit plus qualifying trial and explain tradeoffs.

Research effective proof, case studies, founder credibility, sales calls, objections and ethical exclusivity. Create original sample positioning, outreach and closing language. Do not copy provider ads, imply completed case studies, or promise a specific revenue result.

### 7. Pricing and agency economics

Model $500, $597 and $697 retail against $300 wholesale, plus discovery/grids, outreach tools, payments, client support, founder sales time, reporting, free-trial handling, cancellations, refunds and any membership/setup fee if actually documented. Unknown fees must stay unknown or be explicit sensitivity assumptions.

Provide realistic low/base/high scenarios at 5, 10, 20 and 50 clients. Show contribution versus net operating profit, founder hours, CAC/payback, break-even, maximum sustainable acquisition cost and retention sensitivity. Use scenarios, not invented “industry average” conversions. Cost add-ons before assuming their revenue is profit.

### 8. Retention, delivery and scale

Determine why clients continue or leave after an initial ranking win. Research ongoing service value, the distinction between rank and business outcomes, attribution limits, reporting frequency, client communication, lead quality/handling, support and founder workload.

Consider dependence on fulfillment and territory limits as business constraints. Briefly explain material execution/platform risks, but do not center the assignment on re-proving the provider. No custom software should be necessary to research or start a lean agency.

### 9. Recommendation and launch experiments

Give a candid assessment: worth testing, worth starting under conditions, or unattractive under the current economics. State what supports the conclusion and what remains unknowable before real sales.

Produce a 30/60/90-day launch roadmap, a small first experiment using three free trials, decision criteria, an initial budget and a prioritized list of information worth gathering before joining. The output should leave Mitch ready to evaluate the opportunity, not merely tell him to go obtain tool access.
