## Executive assessment

**Proceed with a tightly controlled 90-day commercial launch test, starting with independent auto repair in Concord, Vallejo and Fairfield.** The most credible positioning is **proof-first local visibility for established businesses**: measure valuable Maps searches on a matched geographic grid, run a no-charge seven-day ranking demonstration for qualified shops, show the same grid after the demonstration, and let the owner choose a monthly continuation.

The strongest differentiator is not simply a free trial or an SEO technique; competitors already offer free trials, rank-before-you-pay and Maps management. The defensible combination is **owner-selected high-value services + independently measured rank improvement + transparent ongoing calls/jobs reporting + proactive account stewardship**. Rank proof earns the initial decision; demonstrable business value earns renewals.

**Updated operator preferences (October 15):**

- **Prospect sweet spot:** businesses currently around **positions 4, 5 or 6** for meaningful, commercially valuable searches in the area that matters, **not businesses already comfortably in the Top 3**. Verify this on a grid, not from an Outscraper export or a single manual search. Rank 4–6 is a preferred commercial target, **not a substitute for provider eligibility** or a universal guarantee of fast improvement.
- **Retention is a central service responsibility.** The founder will make sure owners understand continuing value through clear monthly reporting, qualified-call analysis, booked jobs and client-supplied revenue where available. High retention is a plausible strategic upside for clients receiving real value, **not yet an observed fact or an automatic consequence of ranking gains**.
- **Do not impute a founder hourly wage into CAC or recurring profit.** The owner's hours are not a cash expense for the present decision. Track hours separately as a capacity/process measure, especially as the portfolio grows; do not subtract an arbitrary hourly rate from cash contribution or calculate hourly-based CAC/payback.
- **Evaluate recurring cash contribution and actual cash customer acquisition costs**, with explicit retention sensitivities. A longer-lived client makes acquisition spend easier to recover, but average tenure remains unknown until renewals are observed.
- **Never treat seven-day ranking movement as guaranteed seven-day calls, bookings or revenue.** Outcome reporting is a longer-term retention and value-management system.

These are broad consumer-survey results, not an auto-repair conversion forecast. Google says local visibility depends primarily on **relevance, distance and prominence**, so a business does not have one fixed rank for an entire city. ## 2. Customer selection: rank 4–6, eligible, reputable, commercially valuable

**Start with independent auto repair.** It offers staffed physical locations, review-sensitive Maps buying behavior, a concrete service list, accessible local owners and an existing project dataset. Specialty auto (European, transmission, hybrid), auto body/collision and selected trades are second-wave options. Some plumbing/HVAC/roofing operators use hidden service-area addresses and may fail the provider's public-address requirement.

The provider toolkit's stated trial screening favors:
- Verified, public, staffed physical address on the GBP.
- **10+ positive reviews**, **3.5+ star rating**, relevant linked website.
- **3–5 relevant keywords** under the provider's program.
- Generally **more than 70% of grid points present in Top 20**, average rank approximately **better than 12**, and substantial **remaining Top-3 share opportunity**.
- At least two genuinely relevant qualifying keywords. Exact interpretation of ARP, "not found" points and grid geometry requires provider confirmation.

**Commercial overlay — prefer rank 4, 5 or 6.** The target is an otherwise credible shop sitting just outside the local pack on **the specific high-intent terms and high-value search locations** that its owner wants. A move from fourth to third can be especially easy for an owner to understand. However, one "rank 5" point is not enough: check the **whole grid**, existing Top-3 share, where searchers actually are, and whether the business already dominates its important area. Deprioritize a shop that is Top 3 across most of its commercially useful territory even if an outlying pin is rank 6.

**Do not equate provider coverage with Top-3 success.** A shop can have 100% Top-20 coverage yet almost no Top-3 placements. That can be an ideal opportunity. Conversely, a shop may have an attractive average rank but already hold the Top 3 at its most important pins. Evaluate both metrics.

**Stricter commercial screen:** Prefer roughly **4.5+ stars and 20+ reviews**, or demonstrated review parity with local competitors. This is a heuristic, not a provider rule. Review quality affects click and customer choice after rank improves. See [BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/); broad stated preferences should not be misrepresented as local auto-shop conversion rates.

Additional must-haves: reachable owner, ability to take more work, meaningful margin per desired job, relevant keyword demand, no existing direct competitor conflict, and willingness to pay $597–$697 if the demonstration is persuasive. Get the owner to identify the most valuable services **before** selecting keywords.

Tekmetric's [2026 Shop Index](https://tours.tekmetric.com/answers/tekmetric-shop-index), using data from 10,000+ North American shops on its platform, reported an average repair order near **$704** and median near **$650**. This is *revenue per order, not profit*, and not a random sample of all shops. ## 3. Prospect inventory and independent grids before membership

The existing [Outscraper pilot](OUTSCRAPER_PILOT.md) documented **150 distinct Place IDs**, **96 Concord / 54 Vallejo**, 148 operating at export, and **12 preliminary shortlisted businesses**. Outscraper's returned order is **not** a geo-grid rank. Historical candidates include **The Repair Shop (Concord)**, **Sonoma Auto Tech (Vallejo)**, **Clayton Valley Auto Service**, and **Gene's Auto Repair**. No current rank-4/5/6 finding is established for any of them.

**Lean workflow:**
1. Use Outscraper/Maps category discovery for a fresh list, deduplicated by Place ID; filter closed businesses, chains, poor-fit addresses and weak review parity.
2. Review GBP and linked site, service mix, hours, location, owner access and capacity signals.
3. Run an **independent** geo-grid for a handful of best-fit businesses in each market before paying for provider membership. Historical project tool research listed [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/) ranking grids at around $10 for 2,000 credits (200 trial credits) and [Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/) around $24.99 for 7,500 credits; these are **historical planning quotes**, not current verified offers.
4. Scan **two priority commercial keywords** first. If provider eligibility or activation needs 3–5 total terms, identify those separately; do not pretend two keywords satisfy a stricter provider requirement.
5. A 5×5 grid is 25 measured locations per keyword. Three trials × two terms × two matched scans = **300 grid points**. Confirm each vendor's credit accounting before purchase.
6. Keep business, query, grid center, spacing, number of pins, language, settings and measurement platform identical for before/after. Record timestamp and screenshots/exports.
7. Report Top-3 point share, Top-20 coverage, average rank, location-level movement and any missing points. Avoid selecting only the best-looking pins.

A grid's physical spacing and its total width/radius are not interchangeable. Verify tool geometry rather than assuming a 5×5 grid represents a certain number of miles.

## 4. Acquisition channels and owner outreach

Prioritize **high-specificity founder-led outreach**, not paid ads, until real paid conversion and renewal are demonstrated. This does **not** require assigning a dollar cost to the founder's hours.

| Channel | Priority | Cash spend | Operational attention | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Call with actual grid/audit | Highest | Low | Moderate | Gets owner identity and interest quickly |
| Personalized 45–75 sec video | Highest | Low | Moderate | Makes invisible SEO concrete |
| In-person visit to shop | High | Low travel cash | High | Local credibility, easy owner identification |
| Referral partners (web/IT/marketing) | High after wins | Low–moderate | Moderate | Warm owner introductions |
| Specific follow-up email | High supporting | Low | Low–moderate | Scalable once prospect is qualified |
| Paid Meta/Google ads | Later | Higher | Manageable when proven | Should not fund free trials before funnel economics are known |

Example opener: "I mapped where your shop shows up for [service] around [city]. Your reviews are strong, but on some searches you're just outside the top three. I'm offering a few qualifying local shops a free seven-day ranking demonstration. We show the exact same map before and after, no card or automatic bill. Who's the right person to show it to?"

**Only say "just outside the top three" when the actual scan supports it.** Do not invent "rank 4" for a prospect not yet measured.

Follow-up email after a real scan:

> Subject: [Shop] on Google Maps for [service]  
> Hi [Name] — I checked [service] from 25 locations around [area]. Right now [Shop] appears in the Top 3 at **[actual X] of 25** points; several important areas have you around positions **[actual 4/5/6 where supported]**.  
> I'm offering a qualifying local shop a no-charge seven-day ranking demonstration, then rescanning the identical map. No card or automatic billing. If you want to continue afterward, it's **$697/month, month-to-month**. I made a short audit video. Worth sending over?

Follow applicable email rules, including accurate headers, honest subject lines, contact information and opt-out. [FTC CAN-SPAM](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/controlling-assault-non-solicited-pornography-marketing-act-2003-can-spam-act). Do not send outreach without authorization.

## 5. The seven-day demonstration and trial-to-paid close

**Before activation:** Identify owner, service capacity, desired profitable jobs and target geography. Verify provider eligibility and rank-4/5/6 opportunity on the actual grid. Agree priority keywords and the continuation price. Record baseline. Obtain clear consent, explain that seven days tests **ranking**, not a seven-day sales guarantee, and schedule the results call.

**During:** Let the provider deliver the agreed service. Avoid simultaneously changing many other marketing variables if attribution is a goal. Keep a dated change log.

**At day seven:** Rescan identical points and **all agreed keywords**, not just the best-performing screenshot. Compare baseline versus follow-up Top-3 share and average rank; highlight commercially important points where fourth/fifth/sixth became third or better **only if measured**. Do not overstate a one-week result as a permanent ranking or proven revenue lift.

**Results close:** "For [keyword], you were Top 3 at [X]/25 points and now you're at [Y]/25; your average rank changed from [A] to [B]. That's a measurable improvement in Maps visibility, not a claim that we can prove new revenue in seven days. The ongoing service is $697 per month, month-to-month. We continue building and monitoring visibility, and I send you clear reports on calls, booked jobs and revenue where we can track it. Do you want to keep it going?"

**Objections:**
- "I need calls, not dots." — "Agreed. The grid proves visibility. The monthly service is about translating that into qualified calls and booked work and showing you the evidence."
- "I'm already paying an SEO agency." — "We can measure a specific Maps gap and coordinate rather than replace their broader work."
- "Why keep paying once I'm Top 3?" — "Because competitors move, keywords and territories change, and we're continuing to monitor, defend, expand and connect rankings to your business results."
- "What's the catch?" — "No automatic billing; the continuation price is disclosed upfront; you decide after seeing the evidence."
- "Can you guarantee #1?" — "No one controls Google placement. We guarantee transparent before/after reporting."

Do not pressure a nonbuyer into continuation merely because the provider achieved a rank lift. Use the three free slots to validate **paid customer fit**, not to maximize free trials.

## 7. Retention is an operating system, not an assumed statistic

The founder's intended job is not only getting a shop ranked; it is **keeping the owner aware of the value being created** and helping the business turn additional visibility into profitable work. This is a strong potential retention advantage, especially for rank-4/5/6 shops that newly break into useful Top-3 placements. But clients can still cancel due to weak conversion, lack of capacity, changing competition, budgets, attribution problems or disappointing jobs. **Model retention as a hypothesis until real renewals are recorded.**

**Proposed monthly owner-facing scorecard:**

| Layer | Report | Interpretation guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Visibility | Same-grid Top-3 share by high-intent keyword, average rank, meaningful geographic gains/losses | Grids prove measured visibility, not sales |
| GBP engagement | Calls, website clicks, directions and trend where available | Google's GBP "calls" may be call-button interactions, not answered qualified calls |
| Qualified inquiries | Tracked answered calls, unique callers, service requested, lead quality, missed calls | Obtain consent; exclude spam, duplicates and irrelevant calls |
| Booked work | Appointments, estimates, jobs booked from trackable inquiries | Not all calls turn into work |
| Revenue | Client-supplied closed-job revenue and ideally job contribution margin | Sales totals are not profit; attribution is imperfect |
| Stewardship | Changes made, competitive movement, next keywords/areas, owner action items | Show what ongoing service is doing |

**The client report should answer:** "Where are we newly visible? Are more useful people contacting us? What jobs did that produce? What will we improve next?" A call report alone can be persuasive, but **call volume by itself is not proof that ranking work caused incremental revenue**. Compare with a pre-service baseline when available, separate missed and answered calls, note seasonality, use call tracking/CRM with permission, and invite the owner to validate actual jobs. Avoid inflated ROI claims.

**Suggested retention cadence:**
- **Week 1:** Show rank demonstration and sell ongoing visibility + business outcome measurement.
- **First 30 days paid:** Set up an owner-friendly baseline for calls/inquiries; confirm services the shop most wants and capacity.
- **Monthly:** Send concise scorecard and proactively review wins, misses, next actions; do not leave the owner guessing why they pay.
- **Quarterly:** Review profitable service mix, keyword/geographic expansion, review parity, competitor moves and closed-job value. Refresh goals.
- **If calls fail to improve:** Diagnose search intent, reputation, phone answering, booking process, service area, grid relevance and other channels; do not hide behind green dots.
- **If value is strong:** Ask permission for a case study and referrals, and protect that relationship with consistent communication.

Google's [GBP performance documentation](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en-en) limits how call-button interactions should be interpreted. Any reputation work must use authentic reviews and follow [Google review-content policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114).

**Core $697 scope to validate:** vendor ranking fulfillment, independent monthly grids for priority keywords, competitive monitoring, a clear monthly visibility/outcome report, owner check-in, and reasonable keyword/territory optimization. Price separately for substantial website SEO, landing pages, citation cleanup, paid ads, content production or other unlimited work. Do not erode the $351.09 illustrative cash contribution by silently adding expensive outside fulfillment.

A practical day-90 ambition is **5–10 paying clients if the early evidence supports it**; this is a target, not a forecast.

## 9. Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Rankings move but calls/jobs don't | Pick valuable rank-4/5/6 gaps, reputation-credible shops, track qualified calls and booked work |
| High retention assumed too early | Record actual renewals/churn by cohort; use 6/12/24-month sensitivities as scenarios |
| Free trials attract nonbuyers | Disclose monthly price before activation, qualify buyer/capacity and book result meeting |
| Commoditized SEO competition | Differentiate on independent proof, local owner access and business outcome stewardship |
| Provider dependency | Own customer relationship, grids, reports, prospect pipeline and complementary skills |
| Google policy/algorithm changes | No ranking guarantees or fake/incentivized reviews; compliant supporting services |
| Misleading measurement | Matched independent grids, all agreed keywords, no cherry-picked pins |

The ranking provider's effectiveness is a **premise** for this business assessment. The commercial unknowns are prospect selection, owner access, paid conversion, continuing value and retention.

## Bottom line and handoff

**This agency is worth launching as a measured commercial experiment.** A qualifying auto shop currently around **fourth, fifth or sixth** on valuable searches is a more compelling first customer than one already comfortably in the Top 3.
