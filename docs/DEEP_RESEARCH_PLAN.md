# Deep Research Plan: Selling Google Maps Visibility

**Created:** 2026-10-15 (Pacific)  
**Project:** SomeCallMeMitch/gmb  
**Purpose:** Turn the current concept into an evidence-backed, ethical, repeatable customer acquisition and delivery process. This plan is research-first; it does not treat vendor claims or reseller scripts as validated results.

## Starting point

The project is an agency, not a software product. The likely advantage is a focused prospecting and sales system: find businesses with commercial value and a credible reputation, identify an actual owner or decision-maker, show a specific local visibility gap, and offer a measured, low-friction demonstration. Outsourced fulfillment may provide one service, while our agency owns qualification, sales, client communication, reporting, and durable GBP/website SEO work.

Current working assumptions are $300 per location/month vendor cost and $500/month retail price ($200 contribution before labor, acquisition, tools, support, payment costs, and churn). $597–$697 pricing and $100–$200 add-ons are hypotheses, not validated willingness to pay or profitable offers. The first Outscraper snapshot is 150 auto-repair listings across Concord and Vallejo; its result order is not a rank report. A 12-lead workbook shortlist exists, with The Repair Shop in Concord suggested as a first grid study, but no current geo-grid baseline or lead qualification is recorded.

The attached Jumper Local reseller kit adds a provider's suggested sales and operations framework: qualify visible physical addresses, at least 10 positive reviews, ratings of 3.5+, a keyword-relevant website, 3–5 terms, a seven-day trial, grid metrics (ARP, coverage, Top 3 share), and conversion scripts/ads. Later sections describe local GPS/drive engagement as the mechanism and make strong claims about guaranteed or rapid Top 3 movement. These are provider marketing claims and instructions, not independent evidence. The kit's qualification rules also differ from our broader strategy and from Google's rules for eligible service-area businesses. We should test the useful diagnostic/reporting ideas while independently validating the method, claims, and policy risk.

## Research questions and workstreams

### 1. Customer, market, and prospect selection
- Which local service verticals combine high gross profit per job, meaningful Maps intent, owner accessibility, adequate review credibility, and enough qualified demand?
- Compare auto repair/specialty auto, auto body, HVAC, plumbing, roofing, garage doors, restoration, dental, and other candidates by market evidence rather than assumed attractiveness.
- Define a prospect score with interpretable criteria: review parity against actual top competitors, location/vendor eligibility, geo-grid opportunity, website/GBP improvement potential, service economics, capacity, and verified decision-maker access.
- Establish city/catchment boundaries and validate business coordinates and service area. Avoid overstating what an Outscraper category/city query covers.

**Evidence:** official and trade data where useful; actual local listings and competitor grids; owner interviews; verified lead records.  
**Output:** vertical/city comparison and a versioned prospect qualification rubric.

### 2. Offer and proof
- Determine what a buyer considers valuable: ranking visibility, qualified calls, booked work, GBP support, or a coherent package.
- Compare a free diagnostic, no-charge pilot, paid audit credited toward service, and direct monthly offer. Specify eligibility, duration, scope, permissions, cancellation, and what the client receives.
- Set truthful claims and expectations. Separate observed rank changes from calls, customers, and revenue; define limits of attribution.
- Decide whether to disclose the fulfillment method and how to explain uncertainty and potential Google policy exposure.

**Evidence:** prospect interviews, competitor offers and terms, platform policy, pilot records.  
**Output:** offer brief, proof/report template, claims guide, and objections grounded in evidence.

### 3. Fulfillment vendor diligence and Google policy
- Identify the vendor and obtain written terms, exact methodology, eligibility, trial terms, billing/cancellation, data access/export, branding, service geography, liability, and adverse-outcome handling.
- Independently assess whether coordinated simulated searches, directions, visits, or profile engagement comply with current Google Maps/Business Profile policies and whether they create account/suspension/reputation risk.
- Distinguish legitimate local SEO (accurate profile data, relevant content, genuine reviews and customer activity) from artificial engagement.
- Evaluate a continuity path that can deliver value if the drive-based method stops working or cannot safely be sold.

**Evidence:** current primary Google policy documentation; vendor contract and technical explanation; independent domain expertise; observed controlled outcomes.  
**Output:** written vendor risk/terms checklist and go/no-go criteria. Do not promise rankings, undetectability, policy approval, or guaranteed revenue.

### 4. Rank measurement and causal validation
- Compare current grid providers using the same business, keyword, center, radius, grid, scan time, rank depth, export and reporting needs. Verify live plan pricing/features before purchase.
- Establish reproducible baselines: fixed keyword intent, coordinates, grid spacing, scan settings, date/time, competitors, and snapshots. Use at least 2–3 businesses and include repeated scans or comparison businesses if feasible.
- Measure top-three share, coverage, average rank with missing-result handling, and location-level movement. Report uncertainty and ordinary volatility.
- For any treatment pilot, record other concurrent changes (profile, site, reviews, competitors, seasonality) and compare day 0, 7, 14, and 30 rather than cherry-picking a best screenshot.
- Where authorized, connect rank outcomes to GBP interactions, qualified inquiries, appointments, closed work, and retention.

**Evidence:** provider exports; consistent repeated observations; client-authorized GBP/CRM records.  
**Output:** measurement protocol, matched report, and evidence table separating rank, business outcomes, and causal confidence.

### 5. Decision-maker identification and outreach
- Test how often the actual owner/marketing decision-maker can be verified through business websites, public business records, and human calls; avoid treating a generic inbox or receptionist as the decision-maker.
- Compare concise phone, personalized email, and short screen-recorded audit approaches. Lead with one genuine observation and permission to share the report.
- Test follow-up timing and message variants without unsupported loss claims or pressure tactics. Record opt-outs and comply with applicable outreach rules.
- Track every funnel step: qualified listing → decision-maker verified → audit → contact → reply → conversation → pilot → paid → retained/cancelled.

**Evidence:** small controlled outreach batches, call dispositions, reply/meeting/pilot outcomes, time spent.  
**Output:** tested scripts, contact verification process, and funnel conversion/effort report.

### 6. Pricing and unit economics
- Confirm actual vendor commitment and variable costs, then model $500, $597, and $697 against time, tools, payments, support, refunds, CAC, and retention.
- Cost the add-on work by scope and hours before pricing GBP/site SEO. Test demand and delivery burden separately from the ranking offer.
- Measure CAC per paying client and gross contribution at 30/90/180 days. Do not use annualized contribution as profit or assume retention.

**Output:** transparent unit-economics model with break-even and sensitivity ranges; price test recommendation.

## Sequence and gates

| Phase | Work | Gate to continue |
|---|---|---|
| A. Desk research and diligence | Current Google policies, market/competitor offers, vendor identity and terms, live tool pricing/features | No unresolved critical vendor terms or policy question that makes the offer unsellable |
| B. Measurement setup | Select a grid tool, document fixed scan protocol, refresh and screen 5–10 prospects | Reproducible baselines and at least three credible prospects |
| C. Buyer discovery | Interview 5–10 owners/decision-makers about pain, proof, buying process, alternatives, and budget | Clear buyer language and a concrete testable offer |
| D. Limited field test | Contact 20–30 qualified prospects; run only permissioned pilots with documented baseline and scope | Evidence on decision-maker reach, response, meetings, trial acceptance, outcomes, and effort |
| E. Economics and decision | Review conversion, rank and business outcomes, risk, labor, retention signals, and cost | Scale, revise, or stop based on explicit thresholds; no software build before the manual process repeats |

The repo's existing 30-day plan includes a larger initial outreach target (100+) and a milestone of 30 screened prospects, three baselines, 10 outreach attempts, and one measured pilot. This research plan stages smaller gates first so we can correct the offer and measurement process before increasing volume; update the older plan after choosing the test scope.

## Immediate priorities

1. Get the vendor's legal name/site, written offer and terms, detailed method, eligibility rules, and what exactly the free trial includes.
2. Review current Google policy sources against the method and decide what claims/delivery are acceptable.
3. Re-check current geo-grid tool pricing/features; run baseline scans for 3 shortlisted auto-repair businesses with two genuinely relevant terms each.
4. Verify the businesses, their review parity, address/customer-facing location, owner contact route, and service economics; treat The Repair Shop as a candidate, not an assumed winner.
5. Conduct a small set of buyer interviews and outreach tests before finalizing price or adding a CRM/app.

## Research log discipline

Store findings in the repo with date checked, source link, source type, the exact claim supported, confidence/limitations, and the decision it affects. Label each item as **observed fact**, **user-reported**, **vendor claim**, **external source**, or **hypothesis**. Keep raw exports, private contact details, customer data, credentials, and confidential vendor contracts out of this public repository. Add only appropriate summaries or sanitized evidence.
