## 1. Agency Reseller Guide, pages 3–19

### Dashboard and geography, pages 5–6

The dashboard combines keyword metrics and a geographical ranking grid. Each point represents a search location and displays the business's observed rank there.

- **ARP:** described as the average rank across the grid. Missing-result treatment is not clearly specified.
- **Coverage:** percentage of points where the business appears within the top 20.
- **Top 3%:** percentage of points ranking in positions one through three.
- **Scan date:** when tracking/activity last ran.
- Keywords can be deleted/reset.

The kit names Community and City options. It describes Community as 5 by 5 miles/25 square miles and City as 10 by 10 miles/100 square miles, while also using radius language. Actual side length, radius, spacing and number of pins should be distinguished when choosing a prospecting/reporting workflow. A 5 by 5 point grid and 5 by 5 miles are different specifications.

### Trial eligibility, pages 7–11

The provider calls for:
- Verified physical address publicly listed on the GBP.
- At least 10 positive reviews.
- Rating of 3.5 stars or better.
- Linked website with relevant service content.
- For multi-location clients, a separate landing page with matching address/phone information.

The kit favors a narrow set of **3–5 trial keywords**, with commercial/service and location intent (near me, local, city name, service in city). General targets are:
- ARP around 12 or better; elsewhere worded strictly below 12.
- Coverage above 70%; the positive example uses above 75%.
- Top 3 share under about 50%, leaving room to demonstrate improvement.

It discourages very weak terms and already dominant terms. A low ARP can be misleading if the business appears at very few points.

The standard trial is **seven days**, with improvement in ARP, coverage and Top 3 share as the expected demonstration. It separately notes that Top 3 placement can take 30 days or longer depending on the starting point, competition and content. Therefore, a one-week demonstration should not automatically mean every keyword becomes number one or every area becomes Top 3.

### Active client recommendations, pages 12–13

The reseller guide recommends starting an ongoing Community plan with **10 keywords** and City with **20**, selecting established terms with >70% coverage and roughly ARP 11 or better. It suggests pacing additions and expanding after the first terms improve, often after 30–60 days.

It describes early fluctuations during the first 30–45 days and recommends monthly reporting on:
- Keyword/rank movement.
- New Top 3 wins.
- Next terms to add and why.

**Internal inconsistency:** other sections say 3–5 keywords per client with no exceptions. Treat 3–5 as the clear trial guidance; do not assume the paid-plan allowance until the provider clarifies the plan.

### GBP and website work, pages 14–16

The kit presents the ranking service as complementary to sound SEO/content. Suggested GBP work includes categories, services/descriptions and original images. Suggested website work includes service pages, pages about areas genuinely served, and FAQs.

It attributes slow/N/A terms and geographic plateaus to missing supporting content. Those diagnostic explanations are provider guidance; the new research should assess the business scope and add-on opportunity rather than accept every diagnostic statement as a universal rule.

### Reporting and cancellation, pages 17–19

The guide suggests watermarkable/exportable GIF reporting and comparing rank improvements with GBP calls, clicks, bookings and directions.

It describes cancellation through Client Management, activity ending within 24 hours, and a claimed gradual ranking decline over 20–45 days or faster if a competitor replaces the account. These are provider statements, not observed agency retention/decay data or confirmed contract terms. Use actual ongoing service value to study retention.

## 2. Advertising guidance, pages 20–29

### Offer and audiences, pages 22–24

Core offer: **free seven-day trial**, with no card, commitment, or website/GBP access required for the ranking service; demonstrate first and let the business decide afterward.

The kit describes:
- Meta Advantage+ audiences and placements.
- Adults 25+, all genders.
- 1% lookalikes from paid buyers and qualified leads.
- Business/marketing interests, business-owner/admin behaviors, owner/decision-maker titles, and vertical-related signals.
- Consolidating spend and avoiding excessive restrictions.
- Vertical 9:16 video.

These are the provider's claimed winning settings. A new agency has no buyer/qualified-lead seed list, may serve a limited geography, and cannot assume the same targeting options or results are available. Research current platform options and a realistic small-agency test budget independently.

### Creative approaches, pages 25–29

Three example angles:
1. Founder/CEO addressing local business owners directly.
2. Industry-specific story, exemplified by plumbing.
3. Unusual real-world filming location/pattern interruption.

The examples use free trials, fast ranking demonstrations, proof, ease of setup and sometimes geographic/category exclusivity. They include strong traffic/ranking claims. Those claims should be checked before use in sales copy; they are not agency conversion statistics.

**Usage restriction, page 25:** examples are inspiration only. The kit forbids uploading, editing or running Jumper's videos in a reseller's ad accounts and says copy/headlines are models, not material to take and use directly. Produce original creative and wording.

## 3. Sales process, pages 30–46

The provider organizes the sale around **two calls**:

### Qualification/setter call, pages 31–41

For inbound trial signups, contact quickly (the kit specifies five minutes), confirm address/category/website, choose 3–5 relevant eligible terms, explain the trial, and **book the post-trial results call before ending the conversation**.

It recommends:
- Checking the GBP's main category and website alignment.
- Keeping a narrow keyword list.
- Confirming services with the buyer.
- Booking about a week out.
- Using a computer/screen share for the grid review.
- Repeating the appointment details and providing confirmation/reminders.

The five-minute instruction is an inbound lead-response practice, not a universal cold-prospecting rule. A solo founder may perform both roles.

### Results/closing call, pages 42–46

Have baseline and latest scans available. Revisit the original visibility issue, show the same keyword/point comparisons, invite the owner's reaction, relate the opportunity to their services, explain ongoing work, then ask whether they want to continue at the monthly price.

The script also suggests checking the owner's GBP performance for calls/clicks. Its numerical statements about shares of local traffic are marketing claims, not validated inputs for this project's financial forecast.

The research should adapt the process to a new agency with no case studies, limited trial slots and uncertain buyer access. It should distinguish demonstrated rankings from actual new inquiries and closed jobs.

## 4. Objections and recurring offer, pages 47–57

Topics include:
- Affordability and value for different business sizes.
- Ranking movement in the first week, with downstream leads/revenue potentially later.
- A **90-day ranking guarantee** and possible extra-time credits/refunds.
- No long-term commitment/hidden fees in the sample script.
- Local/category exclusivity and waitlists.
- No need for website/GBP credentials for the ranking service.
- Working alongside the client's existing SEO/marketing provider.
- A dashboard plus check-ins and customer-action reporting.
- Case studies, trust and method explanations.

The exclusivity explanation uses one business per industry/area and category blocking. Advertising examples elsewhere use ZIP/industry wording. Exact territory and whether different keywords/subcategories conflict are not specified sufficiently for a reseller to promise exclusivity.

The dashboard is described as available around the clock, while the reporting answer refers to scans every two weeks. Dashboard access does not establish continuous rescanning. The trial needs appropriate before/after scans.

The kit includes categorical statements about platform safety and adaptation. Fulfillment effectiveness is accepted for this opportunity study, but this document does not independently certify every promotional statement, contract promise or guarantee. Any material risk can receive a concise treatment within a broader commercial assessment; it should not dominate the assignment.

## 5. Keyword and prospect qualification, pages 58–67

Distinguish **keyword qualification** from **prospect qualification**. A prospect with several eligible commercial terms gives the sales conversation more substance than one keyword chosen only because it is easy.

General sweet spot:
- Coverage >70%.
- ARP <12.
- Not already dominating Top 3.
- Real service/category relevance.
- Enough opportunity for a visible short trial.

Page 62 allows exceptions for high-value terms, describing coverage just under 60% and ARP slightly above 14 as sometimes acceptable. These are provider judgment exceptions, not empirically validated cutoffs.

Page 65:
- **2–5 qualifying terms:** book the next call.
- **One valuable term:** borderline; judge service economics, intent and competition.
- **Most terms already dominant:** low opportunity.
- **All terms too weak:** poor rapid-trial fit.

### Metric ambiguity

Some passages disqualify near-100% Coverage as already winning, despite Coverage being defined as top-20 presence. A business can have 100% top-20 coverage, average rank eight and 0% Top 3 share. Do not discard that business solely from coverage. Clarify whether the intended exclusion is high Top 3 share.

## What this means for the agency research

Study the market for a simple sequence: **find a suitable local business → reach its buyer → agree a no-charge demonstration → show a one-week ranking change → sell ongoing service → retain through useful results and service.**

Research demand, competition, qualification yield, customer acquisition, pricing, costs, trial conversion, ongoing value and operational load. The provider's kit supplies the proposed process; independent sources should assess the business opportunity and practical ways to implement it.
