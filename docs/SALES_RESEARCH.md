## 3. Where Outscraper fits

Outscraper remains useful for a broad inventory: business name, website, phone, reviews, category, address, coordinates and Place ID. The existing 150 rows can still seed candidate searches and save repeated discovery work.

It cannot answer the decisive question from the export's returned order: **where does this company appear for this term at these locations?** That requires a grid or comparable searches at explicit coordinates.

### Recommended acquisition workflow

1. Choose a city/catchment and one service category.
2. Run relevant, unbranded searches in the provider's grid tool.
3. Examine businesses with credible reputations and incomplete Top 3 coverage.
4. Review the website and actual service fit.
5. Verify the owner or marketing buyer and their ability to take more work.
6. Select three trial businesses from those qualified prospects.
7. Preserve a dated baseline, run the free trial, and review the result with the buyer.

If the provider's tool only scans a named business, use the existing shortlist as input. If it also exposes competitors, use those results to discover additional businesses. Competitors are observed only within the scan's geography and result depth; they are not a complete census. A shared market grid helps compare businesses on equal geography. A business-centered grid then qualifies each prospect's actual catchment. Recentered grids answer different questions and should not be compared as identical measurements.

**Recommendation:** use the supplied tool first. Buy another grid service only for a concrete missing function: competitor discovery, bulk work, useful export, shareable reports, or convenient repeat scans.

Local Falcon's competitor reporting supports per-business grid views and ranking/review/category comparisons within a scan. Its Sales Enablement workflow takes CSV business names/addresses and appends rank/report fields after bulk scans; enrichment and scan work should not be confused as one negligible lookup. [S1, S10] Whitespark currently lists competitor analysis, CSV/PDF export, sharing, 200 trial credits, and plans from $10/month for 2,000 credits; each scanned grid point uses one credit. These are optional alternatives, not required purchases. [S2]

## 4. Prospect qualification: enough visibility to improve, enough business quality to benefit

### Provider's starting criteria

From the attached kit [S0]:

- Physical address listed on a verified GBP.
- At least 10 positive reviews and 3.5+ rating.
- A linked website that supports the selected services.
- Generally 3–5 focused keywords.
- Typical keyword targets: more than 70% coverage, ARP below 12, and material room to improve Top 3 visibility.
- At least two qualifying terms makes a stronger prospect; one particularly valuable term is a judgment call.
- Trial expectation: seven days to demonstrate movement; reaching Top 3 can take longer.

Use these as provider qualification guidance. Our preferred prospects should also have a strong recent reputation, review parity against the businesses that appear nearby, realistic job contribution, a reachable buyer, and capacity for more work.

### Resolve one metric ambiguity

The kit defines Coverage as the percentage of points ranking in the top 20, while Top 3% measures the top three. Some later passages reject near-100% coverage as if it means no upside. These are different:

| Hypothetical 25-point scan | Meaning | Sales interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 100% top-20 coverage; ARP 8; 0% Top 3 | Visible everywhere, but not among the top three at any sampled point | Potentially excellent opportunity, subject to provider acceptance |
| 100% top-20 coverage; 92% Top 3 | Already among the top three at 23 of 25 sampled points | Limited upside on that exact term and area |
| 32% top-20 coverage; visible-only ARP 4 | Looks strong where found, but missing at 17 points | Poor fit for a rapid trial under standard provider guidance |

Ask the provider to confirm whether the exclusion means high **Top 3 share**, rather than top-20 coverage alone. Also ask how ARP treats missing results. Do not silently equate a provider's ARP with another platform's ARP or ATRP.

Our initial screening tiers should be simple:

- **Ready for trial:** suitable location and website; at least two relevant qualifying terms; credible reviews; buyer engaged; capacity and budget fit; results appointment booked.
- **Needs preparation:** attractive business but weak service content, unclear location suitability, no buyer access, or terms below the provider's threshold.
- **Low priority:** already dominant on the relevant grid, little commercial search relevance, poor customer experience, or no interest/capacity for growth.

These tiers are proposed operating rules, not validated prediction scores.

## 5. Keyword research: commercial relevance before an easy green map

Use terms customers would realistically use to buy services the shop actually performs. Add location variants deliberately. Google Keyword Planner's historical searches depend on location, time range and network and include close variants; its Competition field describes advertisers, not organic Maps difficulty. [S11]

A useful process:

1. Read the business's services, vehicle specialties, and GBP category.
2. Ask the owner which work is most desirable and profitable.
3. Build a small set spanning a core service, a profitable specialty, and relevant location variants.
4. Scan those terms; keep the provider's 3–5 strongest eligible, relevant terms for the trial.
5. If available, use local Keyword Planner data and client-authorized GBP search terms as demand clues. A blank or small-volume estimate is not proof of no demand.
6. Avoid branded searches, unrealistic distant areas, and terms selected solely because they are easy to rank.

## 6. Reach the person who can buy

### Research sequence

1. Business-owned About, team and contact pages; look for ownership and management roles.
2. Public industry/chamber profiles or credible business listings; check consistency of name, location and recency.
3. BAR/DCA license records for identity and business status where useful.
4. California SOS filings for the legal entity and named officers/managers, with role limitations noted above.
5. A brief call to the business to confirm who handles Google visibility and marketing decisions.

Stop researching when a practical verified business route exists. Do not spend an hour searching for a private cell number when the shop will identify the person and their preferred contact method.

A current website may differ from a search snippet or older listing. Record source date and confidence; treat unconfirmed names as research leads. For example, Sonoma's About page supports a family/management-oriented business story, but that does not establish who currently has authority to approve this service. [S12]

### Suggested internal fields

Business/Place ID; source and date; qualifying terms; grid/report link; reputation comparison; buyer name and role; role verified by whom/when; preferred business contact route; desired jobs; capacity; quoted price; stage; next action; opt-out; trial start/end; review appointment; outcome and reason.

Keep the contact list and prospect-specific reports in private working records. Store the general method and aggregate learning in this public repository.

## 7. Selling process and message tests

### The initial offer

A useful offer contains four concrete things:

- An actual map showing visibility on relevant searches.
- A no-charge ranking trial for a qualifying business.
- A scheduled before/after review.
- A clearly stated monthly option if they choose to continue.

Free access removes a financial barrier, but owners still invest attention and trust. Explain what they must do, what you will show, the post-trial price and scope, and whether they must authorize continuation. Avoid surprising them with the price only after a successful trial.

**Proposed message angle:** the owner already has a credible business; help more nearby searchers see it on relevant terms. Use their services and neighborhoods in the conversation. Avoid generic claims about lost revenue or a fixed fraction of all customers going to the top three. No independently established universal traffic percentage was found for the kit's numerical sales claims.

### Proposed first outreach tests

These are draft angles to test, not externally validated scripts or messages already sent.

**Phone opening, before a confirmed scan:**  
“Hi, I'm Mitch. Who handles your Google Maps visibility and decisions about bringing in more repair work? We offer a free ranking trial for qualifying shops. I'd like to check whether your location and services fit.”

**After a genuine scan:**  
“I checked [service] searches across [area]. Your shop has strong reviews, but you appear in the top three at [X] of the [N] locations checked. We can show you the map and offer a free trial to improve that visibility. Would you be open to seeing it?”

**Email body after a genuine scan:**  
“I checked how [Business] appears for [term] across [area]. You're in the top three at [X/N] sampled locations, with room to improve around [actual neighborhood]. We offer a free ranking trial for qualifying businesses and show the before/after on the same map. Would you like to see your results?”

Add accurate sender/business identification, required commercial-email disclosures, postal address and a clear opt-out. FTC guidance covers commercial B2B email, including small campaigns. [S16]

**Short video structure:** identify the term/area/date; show the current grid and one genuine competitor comparison; explain the free trial; invite a response. Test roughly 45–75 seconds as an operating choice, not a proven optimal duration.

### Outreach cadence — proposed, not a benchmark

Day 1: short call or personalized email. Day 3: follow up with one useful map detail. Day 6: second call or short video if appropriate. Day 10: final permission-based follow-up. Stop on refusal/opt-out and log the outcome.

Start with 20–30 qualified businesses. Calls plus email are a reasonable starting hypothesis for owner-operated shops. Record which channel actually reaches the buyer; do not assume email performance benchmarks from enterprise sales apply. Use this batch to learn objections and workflow cost, not to declare a statistically proven winning script.

## 8. Use all three initial trials as sales opportunities

The kit's strongest operational idea is the sequence: qualify and select terms, then schedule the results call before activation. Its immediate-response script is aimed at inbound signups; it is not evidence that an uncontacted cold prospect expects a call within five minutes. [S0]

### Before activation

- Buyer agrees the business and service areas are correct.
- Confirm which jobs they want and whether they can accept them.
- Select the relevant qualifying terms with the provider.
- Show and preserve the baseline.
- State the continuation price, scope and cancellation arrangement.
- Book a screen-share results review for about day 7.
- Agree what will count as a useful demonstrated improvement.

### Results review

1. Restate the agreed terms and geography.
2. Show the same keywords and points before/after.
3. Explain the changed number of Top 3 points and where those points are.
4. Review inquiries or appointments if available; distinguish observed results from hoped-for future business.
5. Ask whether ongoing service fits their goals and quote the agreed monthly option.
6. Record paid, deferred, declined, or no-show and the reason.

**Hypothetical explanation:** 5 of 25 points before and 13 of 25 after means Top 3 share rose from 20% to 52%, an increase of **32 percentage points**. It does not establish 32% more customers or any particular call lift.

A seven-day trial can demonstrate rank movement. Customer acquisition and retention may take more observation. Google's profile Calls metric counts call-button clicks, not completed or qualified calls; its performance totals may include both organic and Google Ads activity. [S17] Client records should establish actual booked work. Because profile activity is part of the described fulfillment, directions/profile interactions alone should not be presented as independent new-customer evidence.

### Common objections: response objective

| Objection | Useful response objective |
|---|---|
| “I already rank.” | Show how the same relevant search varies by location; skip an already dominant term. |
| “My agency handles it.” | Ask what they currently receive; offer a clearly scoped demonstration and avoid attacking the incumbent. |
| “Free usually has a catch.” | State trial duration, actions/access required, price afterward and how continuation is authorized. |
| “I need customers, not dots.” | Agree which work matters; show visibility as the demonstrated result and measure real inquiries separately. |
| “Too expensive.” | Use the owner's own job contribution and acquisition economics; do not invent margins. |
| “I'm too busy.” | Confirm growth capacity; they may be a poor current prospect. |
| “Will results disappear if I stop?” | Explain the actual continuing service; avoid a definite decay timeline without evidence. |

## 9. Price and profit discipline

Keep the first offer simple. At the user-reported $300 monthly fulfillment cost:

| Price/month | Revenue less provider cost | Margin before other expenses |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $500 | $200 | 40.0% |
| $597 | $297 | 49.7% |
| $697 | $397 | 57.0% |

**Recommendation:** use the current $500 working price for the first small cohort if it covers the defined scope, and measure sales/support effort. Treat later pricing tests as willingness-to-pay learning, not immediate proof from a handful of prospects.

A published $1,299/month BrightLocal managed SEO package is a reference for the broader category, not a comparable price for our narrower ranking service. [S18] It does not prove local owners will pay our price.

Use contribution per incremental job: at $200/job, $500 requires 2.5 extra jobs/month to cover the fee; at $500/job, one extra job; at $1,000/job, half a job per month on average. These are arithmetic examples, excluding other client costs and timing.

Tekmetric's June 2026 index reports a $704.25 average repair order across its North American dataset, but repair-order revenue is not profit, a Bay Area quote, or a customer's lifetime value. [S19] Ask each owner for their actual job economics.

For our agency: if a client produces $200/month before other expenses and acquiring them consumes $300 of labor/tools, the simple acquisition payback is 1.5 months **before recurring overhead**. Replace the illustration with tracked costs. Cost add-on delivery before selling ongoing GBP/site work for $100–$200/month.

## 10. Minimum measurement and record keeping

Preserve business/Place ID, exact terms, provider, timestamps, center coordinates, point coordinates/spacing, grid count, geographical extent, result depth, business-open status, handling of absent results, Top 3 share, coverage and ARP definition.

The kit mixes square-mile dimensions with radius language. Confirm actual settings in the tool; do not assume a “5x5” label means the same thing as 5 miles across, a 5-mile radius, or 25 pins. Leave the chosen geometry unchanged for before/after reporting.

Google explains local results partly through relevance, distance and prominence. [S20] Use this to explain why a universal citywide rank is misleading.

A trial can be persuasive without complex software. A prospect log, saved grid reports, and a brief results summary are sufficient.

## 11. Practical next actions

1. Obtain the provider grid tool URL/access or sample exports. Determine whether scanning is available before activation and whether it shows competitor grids.
2. Reuse the current shortlist, scanning a small number of relevant terms for 5–10 businesses rather than buying a larger raw listing export.
3. Review The Repair Shop, Sonoma Auto Tech, Clayton Valley Auto Service and Gene's Auto Repair as initial candidates; choose the final three using actual grids and buyer readiness.
4. Verify contact roles and speak with qualified buyers about desired services and capacity.
5. Book three free trials with results appointments and continuation terms stated in advance.
6. Record qualification yield, buyer reach, meeting show rate, trial-to-paid outcomes, price response, effort and reasons lost.
7. Expand outreach only after reviewing these three experiences.

### Funnel denominators

Track businesses scanned; businesses meeting keyword criteria; buyer roles verified; buyers actually reached; trials offered; trials accepted/activated; results meetings held; paid clients; retained clients. Report counts as well as percentages.

If no owner is reached, improve contact research. If owners engage but decline the trial, inspect offer clarity and trust. If rank results are good but nobody buys, investigate commercial relevance, expectations, price, presentation, and capacity. Three trials reveal practical problems; they cannot establish a reliable long-term conversion rate.

## 12. Remaining research should follow the bottleneck

The desk research is sufficient to design the first sales experiment. The highest-value remaining work is provider-tool inspection and actual prospect scans. After those results, deepen the specific weak point: finding eligible searches, reaching buyers, converting the trial, or retaining clients.

No public-source research can establish current grid eligibility, real owner response, local willingness to pay, or our retention without actual measurements and conversations. The provider's ranking capability remains an accepted working assumption as instructed.
